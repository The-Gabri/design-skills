Workshop note · Porto
  1. 01

    Restraint

    Fewer objects, made better. If a detail doesn’t serve the room, it doesn’t ship. Our catalogue holds forty pieces — most workshops our size hold four hundred.

  2. 02

    Honest materials

    Oak, linen, stone, rattan — finished matte and left to age. No veneer pretending to be solid, no plastic pretending to be wood.

  3. 03

    The beauty of wear

    A softened edge, a darkened handle, a linen throw gone pale at the fold. These aren’t flaws to prevent; they’re the patina the design was waiting for.

  4. 04

    Built to be repaired

    Every joint is mechanical — dovetails, wedges, dowels. If a leg loosens in twenty years, any carpenter can fix it. We’ll even send the wedges.

Materials & care

Kept, not replaced.

Natural materials ask for a little attention and repay it for decades. Here’s the whole manual — it fits on one page.

Wipe with a dry linen cloth; damp only for spills. Re-oil once a year with the soap flakes included in every order. The wood will darken gently where your hands rest — that’s the design, not damage.

It’s pre-washed, so what arrives is the size it keeps. Undyed linen pales softly in sunlight; wash cold, line-dry, and never iron the fold lines out — they’re part of the texture.

Yes. Our cane is woven over a solid oak frame and rated for 120 kg. Keep it out of prolonged direct sun and wipe the weave with a barely-damp cloth twice a year to keep the fibres supple.

Write to the workshop with your maker’s mark number and we’ll post a wedge kit with instructions — free, forever. Every Kanso joint is mechanical, so any carpenter can re-seat it in minutes.
