Our craft

Slow wool,
honest hands.

We have knitted in the same brick building by the harbour since 1962. Twelve knitters, two menders, one very old coffee machine — and no plans to change the recipe.

Our wool comes from spælsau and Norwegian white sheep grazing the hillsides of Vestlandet. Long, strong fibres with a natural water-repellent lanolin — the reason a fjellull sweater shrugs off a Bergen drizzle.

The yarn is sorted and spun at a family mill in the west of Norway, then dyed in small batches. Where we can, we dye with plants: onion skins, birch leaves, madder root. The rest uses low-impact dyes, and we publish the full list — nothing to hide.

Twelve knitters work in our Bergen workshop, most of them for over a decade. Every sweater is knitted in one piece on our flatbed machines, then hand-linked at the shoulders and finished by hand — cuffs, collar, and the little woven label sewn in by Marit, who has done it since 1987.

Each piece leaves with the knitter's initials on the label. If something ever goes wrong, you will know exactly whose hands to blame — and who will fix it.

Free repairs for life. Elbows, moth holes, a cuff worn thin from ten winters of wear — send the piece back and our two menders will fix it, usually within two weeks. Around 400 sweaters a year come home for a second life.

If we cannot fix it, we unravel the wool and spin it into new yarn for our beanies. Nothing from our workshop ends up in landfill — that has been the rule since 1962.

Care & repairs

Good questions.

As rarely as possible. Hang it on the line in fresh air — wool cleans itself, and a night outside removes most smells. When it truly needs a wash: cold water, a drop of lanolin soap, no wringing, dry flat away from the radiator.

Our fit is relaxed, the way knitwear should be. Take your usual size for a comfortable fit, or size down if you like it neat. Between sizes and planning to layer? Take the larger one.
