City Hall

Council Votes 7–2 to Restore the Crosstown Trolley

The $240 million plan would bring rails back to Grand Avenue by 2029, seventy-five years after the last streetcar ran.

By Elena Marsh · City Hall Bureau

CITY HALL — The City Council voted 7 to 2 on Thursday night to approve a $240 million plan to restore streetcar service along Grand Avenue, setting in motion the most ambitious transit project in the city's history since the subway extension of 1987.

The first phase would lay six miles of track from the riverfront to the Central Library, with service expected to begin in the spring of 2029. Supporters called the vote a correction of "the great mistake of 1954," when the council tore out the last rails to make room for automobiles.

"Seventy-five years ago we tore the rails out," said Councilwoman Priya Natarajan, the plan's chief sponsor. "This time we are putting them back on purpose."

Opponents warned that the cost estimates were "aspirational at best" and that the city could not afford another decade of construction on its main commercial street. "Grand Avenue survived the subway," said Councilman Douglas Frye. "It may not survive the trolley."

The plan passed with votes from an unusual coalition of downtown business owners, transit advocates, and the preservation society, which has spent a decade restoring two of the original 1948 streetcars in a warehouse on Ninth Street.

Construction is scheduled to begin in March. The mayor is expected to sign the ordinance on Monday morning.

Schools

District Approves Later Start Times for High Schools

By Tomás Rivera

The school board voted 5 to 4 early Friday to move high school start times to 8:45 a.m., citing a decade of sleep research. The change takes effect next fall and affects 11,000 students.

Athletic directors opposed the measure, warning that later dismissals would push practices into the dark of winter evenings.

Opinion

The trolley vote tests this city's patience

By Marcus Webb · Columnist

Every generation of this city gets one enormous hole to dig in its main street. Our grandparents dug the subway. Our parents widened the avenue. Now it is our turn to lay the rails back down.

The question was never whether the trolley is a good idea. The question is whether we can stand ten years of orange cones to get there.
