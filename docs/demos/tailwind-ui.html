How it works

From push to preview in three steps.

No YAML to hand-write, no staging server to babysit. Tarmac watches your repo and does the boring parts.

01

Connect your repo

Install the GitHub app and point Tarmac at your repository. It reads your Dockerfile or compose file and figures out the topology on its own.

02

Open a pull request

Every PR gets an isolated copy of your stack with a seeded database. The preview URL posts itself as a comment before your linter finishes.

03

Share the link

Designers, PMs, and QA click through the real thing in their browser. You merge with confidence — Tarmac tears the environment down.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

The median across all customers is 38 seconds from push to live URL. The database snapshot restore usually dominates that time — a 2 GB Postgres clone restores in about 11 seconds thanks to copy-on-write storage.

Only from snapshots you explicitly create. Tarmac restores from sanitized snapshots — PII columns are masked by rules you define — and never touches your production database directly.

Anything that runs in Docker. Tarmac reads your Dockerfile or compose file, so Next.js, Rails, Django, Laravel, and friends all work. First-class integrations exist for Vercel-style frameworks and Postgres, MySQL, and Redis.
