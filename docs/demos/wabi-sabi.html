How a bowl
is born

We dig it an hour from here, from a riverbank nobody photographs. It is full of small stones and iron. We take out the worst of the stones and leave the iron — the iron is what makes the spots.

Three days in the wood kiln, stoking every twenty minutes through two nights. The flame decides the surface: ash lands where it lands. About one piece in six comes out warped, cracked, or strange. Those are usually our favorites.

When a bowl breaks — and good bowls do break — we join the pieces with lacquer and powdered gold. Kintsugi. The seam stays visible forever, because the break is part of the bowl's story now, not the end of it.
